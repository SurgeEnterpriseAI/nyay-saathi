Free legal help websites in India: what each one does

India has several free, official or non-profit services for people with a legal problem. They do different jobs, so the right one depends on what you need: a free lawyer, advice, plain-language explanations of the law, a consumer grievance, government information, or your court date. This guide says what each one does and where Nyay Saathi fits.

NALSA and the Legal Services Authorities: a free lawyer and Lok Adalats

nalsa.gov.in · helpline 15100

The National Legal Services Authority (NALSA) was set up under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987 to give free legal services to weaker sections of society and to organise Lok Adalats, where disputes are settled by agreement. Each state has a State Legal Services Authority, and the District Legal Services Authority (DLSA) at your district court is where you apply for a free lawyer.

Use it when you need a lawyer and cannot afford one, or you want to settle a dispute at a Lok Adalat. See who can get a free lawyer.

Tele-Law: talk to a panel lawyer for advice

tele-law.in

Tele-Law is run by the Department of Justice with CSC e-Governance Services India Limited, with NALSA as a partner. It connects people with panel lawyers for legal advice through Common Service Centres (CSCs) and the Citizens' Tele-Law mobile app.

Use it when you want to speak to a lawyer for advice, for example from a CSC near your village.

Nyaaya: plain-language explainers of Indian laws

nyaaya.org

Nyaaya is an open-access digital resource with legal explainers grouped by theme (such as family and marriage, or police and courts), guides, videos, and sample forms and templates, with Hindi and Kannada versions. Through "Ask Nyaaya" you can send a legal question to be answered by experts and volunteers.

Use it when you want to read about a law and your rights in depth.

National Consumer Helpline and e-Daakhil: consumer problems

consumerhelpline.gov.in · helpline 1915 · edaakhil.nic.in

The National Consumer Helpline is run by the Department of Consumer Affairs. You register a grievance (by phone on 1915, on the portal, or on its app), it is forwarded to the company, and you track the status online. If that does not solve it, you can file a complaint with the District Consumer Commission yourself, without a lawyer, on e-Daakhil.

Use it when a seller or service provider has cheated you or will not refund. See the consumer complaint letter.

RTI Online: information from a government office

rtionline.gov.in

Under the Right to Information Act, 2005 you can ask a public office for information, such as the status of your pension, ration card or application. RTI Online takes applications to central government offices; for state offices, many states have their own portal, or you send it by post to the Public Information Officer.

Use it when a government application is stuck. See the RTI application format.

e-Courts services: your case status and next hearing date

services.ecourts.gov.in

Enter the 16-character CNR number from any court paper to see the next date, the stage of the case and orders. The free eCourts Services mobile app does the same. Without a CNR you can search by party name.

Cyber crime portal and 1930: online fraud

cybercrime.gov.in · helpline 1930

For online fraud or cyber crime, call 1930 immediately and report at cybercrime.gov.in.

Where Nyay Saathi fits: a starting point that sends you to the right one

Nyay Saathi is a free, open-source legal-help guide. It works alongside the services above, not instead of them. You describe what happened in your own words, in English or Hindi, and it:

  1. works out which of eight common problems you have (unpaid wages, consumer fraud, violence at home, police not filing an FIR, cheque bounce, a stuck government application, court case status, land or tenancy);
  2. gives sourced next steps with the right helpline and official link from the list above;
  3. checks whether you can get a free lawyer under section 12 of the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987;
  4. drafts the letter you need: a legal aid application, an RTI application, a consumer complaint, a demand for unpaid wages or a complaint to the Superintendent of Police.

No sign-up, no tracking cookies, and nothing you type is saved. It does not assign you a lawyer, file anything for you or track your case: for those it points you to NALSA, the DLSA, the consumer helpline, RTI Online or e-Courts.

Questions people ask

Which free website helps me get a free lawyer in India?

Free lawyers come through the Legal Services Authorities set up under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987: call the NALSA helpline 15100 or visit the District Legal Services Authority at your district court. Nyay Saathi checks whether you fall in a section 12 category and drafts the application for you to take there.

Is there a free website that drafts legal letters in India?

Nyay Saathi drafts five fill-in letters for free: an application for free legal aid, an RTI application, a consumer complaint to the seller, a written demand for unpaid wages and a complaint to the Superintendent of Police when the FIR is not registered. Nyaaya also publishes sample forms and templates.

Where can I read about my legal rights in simple language?

Nyaaya (nyaaya.org) publishes plain-language explainers of Indian laws, with Hindi and Kannada versions. Nyay Saathi gives short, sourced next steps for eight common problems.

How do I talk to a lawyer for advice without going to court?

Tele-Law, run by the Department of Justice, connects people with panel lawyers through Common Service Centres and the Citizens' Tele-Law mobile app. You can also call 15100 for legal aid.

Do I need to sign up to use Nyay Saathi?

No. There is no sign-up and no tracking cookies, and nothing you type is saved.

Is Nyay Saathi a replacement for a lawyer?

No. It gives general legal information, not legal advice. For your case, talk to a lawyer; legal aid lawyers are free if you qualify (call 15100).
